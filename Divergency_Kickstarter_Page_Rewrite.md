# Divergency: A Dark Fantasy Pixel-Art Beat 'Em Up

Marseille is humanity's last city. Beneath it, a grieving warlord is building a body for an ancient power to save his daughter.

Divergency is a 2D/2.5D old-school beat 'em up adventure with fast combat, character growth, tactical skill selection, commandable allies, and hand-crafted pixel art. It is built for players who miss the energy of arcade brawlers, but want a darker world, deeper combat choices, and a story with weight.

## What Is Divergency?

Divergency is a dark fantasy action game inspired by 90s beat 'em ups, arcade brawlers, platform-action pacing, and modern skill-based combat.

The strongest references are Little Fighter 2 and Hero Fighter for readable chaos, team fights, special moves, and ally command energy; M.U.G.E.N for character variety and experimental matchups; arcade classics like Final Fight, Streets of Rage, Cadillacs and Dinosaurs, and The Punisher for 2.5D lane-based brawling; and modern brawlers/action RPGs like Dragon's Crown, Dungeon Fighter Online, Castle Crashers, and Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles: Shredder's Revenge for character identity, co-op energy, and replayable combat.

Divergency is not trying to clone one of those games. The goal is to take the fast readability of classic brawlers, the freedom of character-driven fighting games, and the darker campaign structure of an action adventure.

You control a small cast of fighters, each with their own rhythm, weapons, movement, and combat style. Between stages, you select a limited skill loadout and passive setup to prepare for the next map. In battle, you read enemy formations, chain combos, manage spacing, command allies, and survive encounters where every enemy type asks for a different answer.

The first campaign release focuses on Act 1: a journey through Marseille, the last industrial city of the known world, and the hidden laboratory beneath its waters.

## Gameplay

### Fast Beat 'Em Up Combat

Combat is built around movement, impact, and mastery. You push through enemy groups, chain attacks, dodge pressure, and use each fighter's strengths to control the fight.

Image caption: Work-in-progress combat capture showing combo flow and enemy pressure.

### Skill Selection

Characters can learn a larger skill pool over time, but they cannot bring everything into a mission. A fighter may have around 10 available skills, while only 4 active skills can be equipped and performed during a stage.

Before each map, you choose the 4 skills and passive upgrades that fit the situation: crowd control for enemy waves, mobility for platform-heavy routes, guard break tools for shield formations, burst damage for bosses, or defensive options for dangerous zones. The goal is preparation, not menu complexity: players should look at the mission, choose a plan, and feel the difference in combat.

Image caption: Work-in-progress skill selection UI mockup.

### Squad Command System

Some battles are not won by one fighter alone. Divergency includes a player-to-AI command system that lets you direct commandable allies during combat.

You can select a nearby General, give quick directional orders, change formation, and trigger assist actions:

- Free: release the squad back into normal behavior.
- Come: regroup around the player.
- Stay Left / Stay Right: hold a flank position.
- Line / Wedge / Circle: change squad formation.
- Assist commands: call the selected General to perform attack, movement, or defense support.

This gives fights a tactical layer without turning the game into a strategy game. The player still brawls in real time, but can shape the battlefield by pulling allies back, holding space, or calling a decisive assist at the right moment.

### Enemies That Force Decisions

Human enemies fight with structure: shield units protect gunners, ambushers try to flank, and stronger enemies force you to break formations before committing to long combos. When allies are present, your command choices matter: regroup before a trap, hold a flank to control space, or call an assist to break pressure.

Demonic enemies fight differently. They are less afraid of death, more direct, and often harder to interrupt. Their role is to make the player adapt instead of repeating one combo forever.

## Story

The known world is dying.

For centuries, the Great Calamity has consumed civilization wave after wave. To the east, only ruins remain. To the west stands Marseille, an industrial empire and humanity's final stronghold.

Inside the city, survival has a price. Outside it, survival is almost impossible.

Jamerson Obsworth was once a decorated officer. Now he rules the city's underworld from the shadows. To most people, he is a tyrant. To himself, he is a father trying to save his daughter, Heniana, who lies trapped between life and death.

Jamerson believes he has found the answer: The Eye, a living fragment of a forgotten god. It offers knowledge beyond human limits. In exchange, it wants a body.

## Act 1: The Marseille Hunt

You are the Stranger, a nameless survivor pulled from Jamerson's prison by Henry, a man haunted by his own past. Alongside Deep and Solei, you escape Bastonne and enter the rotten heart of Marseille.

Your mission is simple: find Jamerson and stop him.

The trail leads beneath the city, through black markets, drowned corridors, and a submarine laboratory where prisoners are no longer treated as human. Some experiments fail and become horror. Others succeed and become something worse.

As the laboratory collapses, Jamerson's voice follows you through the dark:

> "You destroy the only thing that can save her. What are you fighting for?"

For a moment, the question lands.

Because Jamerson believes it. Every crime, every sacrifice, every life broken in that laboratory was committed for one child.

You destroy the facility, but Jamerson escapes. In the ruins, you face a perfect failure: a living preview of what he is trying to create. It leaves behind a symbol from the east, older than Marseille and older than the Calamity.

You did not stop Jamerson. You only slowed him down.

He is not building weapons. He is building a body.

## Characters

### The Stranger

A nameless survivor pulled out of Jamerson's prison. The Stranger is the player's doorway into Marseille, its violence, and the truth hidden beneath the city.

### Deep

Deep was once treated like a hero: a crusader sent into impossible wars, a defender people pointed to when they wanted to believe humanity could still survive. The legend cost him everything. Now older, wounded, and tired of being used as a symbol, Deep wants peace, but Marseille and the Calamity keep pulling him back into violence.

### Solei

Deep's niece, born between cultures and raised in a world that rarely gave her a safe place to belong. Solei carries an Asian heritage in her visual identity and discipline, but she is not written as an exotic stereotype. She is quick, sharp, impatient, and fearless: a speed fighter who strikes before enemies can read her.

### Jamerson Obsworth

A decorated officer turned underworld ruler. Jamerson is cruel, brilliant, and convinced that every horror he creates is justified if it brings his daughter back.

### The Eye

A living fragment of a forgotten god. It whispers, watches, and offers impossible knowledge. It does not want money, land, or worship. It wants a body.

### The Perfect Failure

Jamerson's "perfect failure": an escaped experiment that proves resurrection is becoming real. It is not the final body The Eye wants, but it is close enough to show that Jamerson's work is no longer only theory.

## Risks And Challenges

Divergency is an ambitious action game made by a small team. The main risks are schedule, scope, technical stability, and fulfillment.

### Development Delays

Combat-heavy games need iteration. Animation timing, hitboxes, enemy behavior, UI clarity, and difficulty balance can all take longer than expected. To reduce risk, the base goal focuses on Act 1 instead of promising the entire long-term game at once.

### Scope Control

The full world of Divergency is larger than the first release. The campaign is structured around a focused Act 1 release, with stretch goals clearly separated. If a stretch feature threatens the main release, we will prioritize the Act 1 campaign first.

### Online Features

Online co-op and PvP are harder to deliver than local or single-player content. These features are stretch goals and will be tested before being locked for launch. If stability requires more time, we will update backers instead of hiding the problem.

### Platform And Release Approvals

Any release platform can require technical checks, store page review, or certification. The current plan is PC-first for the Act 1 release, with additional platforms considered after the base experience is stable.

### Physical Reward Fulfillment

Posters and bracelets require production, packing, address collection, and shipping. To keep this manageable, physical rewards are EU only at launch, with estimated shipping ranges shown before backers pledge.

### Communication

If the project hits delays or roadblocks, we will post clear updates explaining what happened, what is being fixed, and how it affects delivery estimates.
