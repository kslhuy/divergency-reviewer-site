# Divergency: A Dark Fantasy Pixel-Art Beat 'Em Up

Divergency is a 2D/2.5D pixel-art beat 'em up adventure with fast combat, skill loadouts, commandable allies, handcrafted stages, and a full dark fantasy campaign.

Lead a small team through a dark fantasy adventure across many stages: prison breaks, street fights, hidden routes, corrupted wildlands, ancient relic sites, and brutal boss battles, all tied to a story about stopping a father who is sacrificing others for one impossible miracle.

Back Divergency to help finish the full game: core combat, playable fighters, enemies, bosses, story stages, music, sound, UI polish, QA, and digital release.

## The Game

Divergency is a dark fantasy action brawler built around readable 2D/2.5D combat, limited skill loadouts, commandable allies, and handcrafted pixel-art stages.

### Inspiration Games

Divergency is inspired by games that made 2D action feel fast, readable, chaotic, and character-driven. These references explain the combat feeling, not the final art direction or story identity. Divergency still has its own dark fantasy world, relic-fragment campaign, and party-based systems.

Classic arcade brawlers like Final Fight and Streets of Rage also guide the 2.5D lane pressure, enemy waves, and stage pacing. Modern brawlers and action RPGs guide replayability, character identity, and loadout depth.

You control a small cast of fighters, each with their own rhythm, weapons, movement, and combat style. Between stages, you choose a limited skill loadout and passive setup for the next map. In battle, you chain attacks, manage spacing, command allies, and adapt when enemy groups change the pressure.

The campaign is a full adventure across many stages. Each chapter adds new enemies, bosses, environments, traversal problems, party moments, and story turns while keeping the focus on fast brawler combat.

## Gameplay

### Fast Beat 'Em Up Combat

Combat is built around movement, impact, and mastery. You push through enemy groups, chain attacks, dodge pressure, and use each fighter's strengths to control the fight.

![Boss encounter in Akam Meskul](<imgs/Stage4/blood_lari_bos.png>)

*Work-in-progress boss encounter capture showing a dark fantasy battle space, enemy pressure, and stage atmosphere.*

### Skill Selection

Characters can learn a larger skill pool over time, but they cannot bring everything into a mission. A fighter may have around 10 available skills, while only 4 active skills can be equipped and performed during a stage.

Before each map, you choose the 4 skills and passive upgrades that fit the situation: crowd control for enemy waves, mobility for platform-heavy routes, guard break tools for shield formations, burst damage for bosses, or defensive options for dangerous zones. The goal is preparation, not menu complexity: players should look at the mission, choose a plan, and feel the difference in combat.

### Squad Command System

Some battles are not won by one fighter alone. Divergency includes a player-to-AI command system that lets you direct commandable allies during combat.

You can select a nearby General, give quick directional orders, change formation, and trigger assist actions:

- Free: release the squad back into normal behavior.
- Come: regroup around the player.
- Stay Left / Stay Right: hold a flank position.
- Line / Wedge / Circle: change squad formation.
- Assist commands: call the selected General to perform attack, movement, or defense support.

This gives fights a tactical layer without turning the game into a strategy game. The player still brawls in real time, but can shape the battlefield by pulling allies back, holding space, or calling a decisive assist at the right moment.

### Enemy Variety

Enemies are built to change how you fight, not only to add more bodies on screen.

Human groups use shields, guns, ambushes, and flanks. You may need to break a formation before committing to a long combo. Demonic enemies are less defensive and more direct: they push through damage, hit harder, and force cleaner movement.

When allies are present, squad commands become part of the answer: regroup before a trap, hold a flank, or call an assist to break pressure.

## Characters

![Divergency main character lineup](<imgs/chars/Poster.png>)

### Stranger / Ghost

A nameless survivor pulled out of Jamerson's prison. The team calls him Ghost after Bastonne because the system cannot fully identify him. He is not a chosen one. He is a survivor of a mistake, and that mistake may be why the relic fragments cannot control him like everyone else.

### Deep

![Deep character art](<imgs/chars/Deep.png>)

Deep was once treated like a hero: a crusader sent into impossible wars, a defender people pointed to when they wanted to believe humanity could still survive. The legend cost him everything. Now older, wounded, and tired of being used as a symbol, Deep wants peace, but Marseille and the Calamity keep pulling him back into violence.

### Solei

Deep's niece, born between cultures and raised in a world that rarely gave her a safe place to belong. Solei carries an Asian heritage in her visual identity and discipline, but she is not written as an exotic stereotype. She is quick, sharp, impatient, and fearless: a speed fighter who strikes before enemies can read her.

### Henry

A former war hero who chose the shadows after learning how easily heroism becomes permission to control other people. Henry gathers information, commands under pressure, and keeps asking whether fighting can still protect people without becoming another excuse for violence.

### Tulas

A mage who controls blood and liquid. Tulas can create shields, platforms, filters, and temporary paths, but his power always touches the boundary of the body. His arc asks whether a frightening ability can be used to save people without turning him into what they fear.

### Block

A loyal ally captured before the story begins. Saving Block in Bastonne is not only a rescue objective; it is the first proof that command and teamwork in Divergency are about trust, not treating allies as tools.

### Heni

A child found in Sakuri with the same face as Heniana. Heni is not written as a key, replacement, or copy. Her role pushes the story's central question: no person should be reduced to material for someone else's miracle.

### Jamerson Obsworth

A decorated officer turned underworld ruler. Jamerson is cruel, brilliant, and convinced that every horror he creates is justified if it brings his daughter back.

### The Relic Fragments

The Eye, The Ear, The Tongue, The Heart, and The Umbilical Cord are living fragments of a Primordial God killed and divided in ancient times. They do not behave like simple artifacts. They see, hear, speak, beat, call, and try to return to one another.

### The Perfect Failure

Jamerson's perfect failure is an escaped experiment that proves resurrection is becoming real. It is not the final body The Eye wants, but it is close enough to show that Jamerson's work is no longer only theory.

## Story

The story begins with the Stranger, a nameless prisoner who wakes during the Bastonne lockdown when he should be unconscious. Deep's team came to rescue Block from Jamerson's prison facility, but the Stranger's impossible survival changes the escape. The team later calls him Ghost, because Jamerson's systems cannot fully read him and no one knows who he used to be.

The known world is breaking. For centuries, the Great Calamity has consumed civilization wave after wave. Marseille presents itself as the last clean stronghold of order, but its survival is built on prisons, missing people, and experiments hidden below the city.

Jamerson Obsworth was once a decorated officer. Now he rules the city's underworld from the shadows. To most people, he is a tyrant. To himself, he is a father trying to save his daughter, Heniana, who lies trapped between life and death.

Jamerson believes he has found the answer: The Eye, a living fragment of a forgotten god. It offers knowledge beyond human limits. In exchange, it wants a body.

### Stage 1: Marseille Unstable

After Bastonne, the team does not plan to attack the laboratory. They plan to cut the trail, hide the vehicle, rest at Armorlite, and leave Marseille before dawn.

Jamerson moves faster.

The safe route is burned. Police and underworld gangs close the surface roads. Armorlite is attacked. Henry changes the escape plan: the team must go below Marseille, through old maintenance tunnels, abandoned metro lines, the black-market undercity of Laundel, and the sewer routes used by people the city refuses to see.

At the port, the promised escape boat is empty. Marius Vane, Jamerson's right hand in the secret police, springs the trap. After the fight, the team learns that prisoners, evidence, and experiments have been moved to a research submarine offshore.

Running away would let Jamerson erase everything.

The escape becomes a sabotage mission. In the submarine laboratory, the team finds prisoners connected to machines, hybrid bodies, cell samples tied to Heniana, and experiments using the biological structure of the Primordial God's fragments. The boss of Stage 1 is Jamerson's perfect failure: not the final vessel The Eye wants, but close enough to prove the ritual is no longer theory.

The submarine collapses. Jamerson survives through a substitute body and escapes with data pointing east toward Sakuri. By the end of Stage 1, the team understands the truth: Jamerson is not building weapons.

He is building a body.

### The Full Campaign

Stage 1 is only the first major step. Divergency continues through five main stages, each built around a fragment of the Primordial God and a different way people justify cruelty.

- Stage 2: Sakuri, where The Ear hears voices, thoughts, sickness, and secrets from far away. Solei confronts identity, belonging, and the danger of turning a child into a sacred tool.
- Stage 3: Calvaria, where The Tongue can speak with the voices of the dead. Deep and Henry face the cost of old wars, false comfort, and the temptation to let the dead command the living.
- Stage 4: Akam Meskul, where The Heart amplifies emotion into violence. Tulas and Block face the line between protection, body horror, loyalty, and survival.
- Stage 5: The Cradle, where every previous stage returns as memory, temptation, and final test. The team must stop Jamerson without repeating his logic: no one should become material for a miracle.

The full campaign is about refusing the easy lie that love, grief, faith, history, or survival can make sacrifice clean.

## Art And World

Every frame of Divergency is built around pixel-art craft: ruined industry, cursed wilderness, hand-animated fighters, grotesque experiments, sacred ruins, and small environmental details that reward players who look closely.

The world mixes traditional beauty with technological decay. Marseille is not a shining capital. It is a machine that survived by feeding on refugees, prisoners, and anyone too weak to resist. Later stages push the same contrast into Sakuri's sacred peninsula, Calvaria's city of graves, Akam Meskul's cursed mountain war zone, and the dreamlike final space of The Cradle.

## Risks And Challenges

Divergency is an ambitious action game made by a small team. The main risks are schedule, scope, technical stability, and fulfillment.

### Development Delays

Combat-heavy games need iteration. Animation timing, hitboxes, enemy behavior, UI clarity, and difficulty balance can all take longer than expected. To reduce risk, the base goal focuses on the core story campaign first: the main stages, core cast, main bosses, and systems needed to finish the game.

### Scope Control

The full world of Divergency has room for more modes, side content, and playable variations than a small team should promise all at once. The campaign is structured around the complete main story first, with stretch goals clearly separated. If a stretch feature threatens the main release, we will prioritize the full core campaign.

### Online Features

Online co-op and PvP are harder to deliver than local or single-player content. These features are stretch goals and will be tested before being locked for launch. If stability requires more time, we will update backers instead of hiding the problem.

### Platform And Release Approvals

Any release platform can require technical checks, store page review, or certification. The current plan is PC-first for the full game release, with additional platforms considered after the base experience is stable.

### Physical Reward Fulfillment

Posters and bracelets require production, packing, address collection, and shipping. To keep this manageable, physical rewards are EU only at launch, with estimated shipping ranges shown before backers pledge.

### Communication

If the project hits delays or roadblocks, we will post clear updates explaining what happened, what is being fixed, and how it affects delivery estimates.
